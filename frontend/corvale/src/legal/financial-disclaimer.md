## Corvale is a tool, not an adviser

Corvale helps you record what happened to your money and shows you what your own numbers add up
to. That is all it does.

Nothing in Corvale is financial, investment, tax, or legal advice. We are not financial advisers,
planners, brokers, accountants, or fiduciaries, and using Corvale does not create any advisory
relationship between you and us.

## Corvale never touches your money

Corvale does not hold, move, transfer, invest, or take custody of funds. It has no connection to
your bank. Every number in it comes from something you typed in or imported yourself.

Two features are worth calling out because they are easy to misread:

- **Adding to your saver** moves no money and creates no transaction. It lowers the spendable
  balance Corvale displays — an earmark to remind you that amount is set aside.
- **A rollover** — the feature the app calls **Pushover** — moves no money either. It snapshots
  your saver balance into history and resets the saver to zero.

Your bank account is untouched by both.

## The numbers that look like predictions

Several features calculate figures that can read like guidance. They are arithmetic applied to
assumptions, and the assumptions will not hold exactly.

**Debt payoff schedules.** Snowball and avalanche plans assume fixed balances, interest rates and
payments, and ignore fees and promotional rates. Real accounts rarely behave that way.

**Cash-flow forecasts.** Projected balances are built from your recurring bills, scheduled goal
contributions, and recent average spending. One unexpected transaction moves them.

**Savings goal projections.** Completion dates assume your recent contribution rate continues
unchanged.

**Report averages and savings rates.** These describe the date range you selected. They are not
forecasts.

**Detected subscriptions.** This list is inferred from repeating patterns. It can miss an
irregular charge or flag a one-off repeat, so treat it as a starting point.

**Budgets.** Budget progress describes what you have recorded against a limit you set yourself.
It is not a recommendation about what you should spend.

### Where you will not see a warning

The dashboard, the budget pages, the calendar and the report builder carry no note like the ones
above, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight. Those screens only total figures you
already recorded — they describe what happened, they do not project what will. Wherever Corvale
does project something, the screen says so.

## Accuracy is your side of the bargain

Corvale can only be as accurate as what goes into it. Missing transactions, typos, or a bank file
that imported oddly will all produce confident-looking numbers that are wrong.

**We give no guarantee that imported data is complete or correct.** Bank and card files come in
many shapes, some of them undocumented and some of them inconsistent between statements. Corvale
parses them on a best-effort basis, and a file can be misread — a wrong sign, a mangled date, a
duplicated row, a column mapped to the wrong field, or a transaction skipped entirely.

**You remain responsible for reviewing imported data before relying on it.** Check it against your
own statements, and reconcile your accounts periodically. That feature exists precisely because
imports and manual entry both drift. Your bank's own statement is the authoritative record, not
Corvale's copy of it.

## Before you decide something important

Talk to a qualified professional — a licensed financial adviser, an accountant, or a tax
specialist — before making a decision that matters. They can account for your full circumstances
in a way that a personal ledger cannot.
